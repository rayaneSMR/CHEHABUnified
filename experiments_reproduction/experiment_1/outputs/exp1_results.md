## Interpretation (empirical fit, not a guarantee)

- **Dominant feature.** Multiplicative depth has Pearson r = 0.9994 with measured noise, and its NNLS coefficient is 33.38 bits per level. NNLS gives non-zero weight only to Multiplicative depth, Expression depth, Negations. Additions, subtractions and ciphertext multiplications are correlated with the depth features, so their raw correlations with noise should not be read as independent effects.
- **Fit quality.** In-sample MAE 2.24 bits, RMSE 3.01, R2 0.9851. The supplementary 5-fold CV gives MAE 2.24, RMSE 3.01, R2 0.9851, close to the in-sample values, so with three non-zero parameters on this many rows there is no sign of overfitting. Rank agreement between estimate and measurement is Spearman rho = 0.892 (the measured values take only 35 distinct integer values, so there are many ties).
- **Direction of error.** 72.4% of rows are underestimated, by at most 7.8 bits (427 rows by more than 5 bits). Underestimation is the direction the spec flags as risky for decryption failures; the figure above is an empirical in-sample worst case, not a bound. The largest overestimates come from the 2 budget-exhausted row(s), whose measured consumption is only a lower bound, so they are not true overestimates.
- **Coverage limits.** 97.3% of rows have multiplicative depth <= 3 and only 10 rows have depth >= 7, the range where tight budgets matter. `rotate_rows` is never non-zero, and `multiply_plain` / `negate` are non-zero in only 15 / 26 rows, so their zero (or, for negate, small) coefficients reflect scarce data rather than evidence of no effect.
- **Usefulness for search guidance.** The fit is tight where the data lives and is cheap to evaluate, which supports using it as an empirical structural proxy to screen and rank expressions. It does not establish that estimated noise bounds or predicts real execution noise, and the evidence is thin at high multiplicative depth.
